# Taste profile

**IS**
- *Live* — things visibly tick; numbers update in place with a short, mechanical flip; a clock is always running.
- *Exact* — tabular figures, hairline rules, prices to the tenth of a cent, tick numbers shown; nothing vague or rounded for effect.
- *Ringside-close* — physical, warm-blooded: corner colours, rope lines, chalk, the bell. You are next to the action, not in a trading terminal.

**NEVER**
- *Casino* — no gold coins, slot-machine glow, confetti, "WIN BIG" energy.
- *Crypto-template* — no purple-to-blue gradients, glassmorphism cards, neon on black with one acid accent, floating 3D coins.
- *Spreadsheet* — no dense grey trading-terminal grids as the face of the product.

**Audience taste.** Uses live-sport apps (scores, broadcast graphics), exchange order books, Solana wallets. Trust = precise numbers that move for a reason, visible proof (tx links, hashes). Excitement = the price line reacting at the moment of a hit.

**Brand anchors.** None exist; everything is new. Plan text asks for "dark broadcast style, big odds line, crisp motion"; treated as a strong hint, not a brand.

**Feel in motion.** Mechanical and quick: split-flap flips (120–180 ms), price ticks that settle with a tiny overshoot, odds line drawing continuously at 10 Hz; heavy moments (KO, bell, reveal) get one slower beat (600–900 ms). No floaty easing.

**Material and texture.** Chalk on slate board, printed ticket stock, broadcast glass graphics with hard edges, rope fibre, stamped ink.

**Taste anchors (to be confirmed by scouting).** Broadcast scorebugs (boxing/UFC/F1 TV graphics) for density and hierarchy; split-flap departure boards for number motion; racecourse bookmaker chalkboards for the odds as handwritten-but-exact; exchange ladders for back/lay clarity.

**Lines not to cross.** Never hide the price behind animation. Never show a number that did not come from chain or results.json. No fake "live users" counters. Gambling-harm tone: no pressure copy.

**Comfort.** Watched at night next to a TV or laptop, often on a phone in one hand; long sessions during a match. Dark ground is justified by night viewing and broadcast context; one light direction is still explored for /docs and /proof readability.
